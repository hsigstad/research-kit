# Institutional / background sections

Loads on top of [`../writing_style.md`](../writing_style.md) (general
baseline) and [`body.md`](body.md) (body baseline).

---

- **State institutional facts as facts.** Cite primary sources (statutes,
  case law, official documents). Verify against
  `research/institutions/brazil/` (or analogous reference) — see
  `validate-section`'s `institutional_claim_check`.
- **Define every non-English term on first use.** A reader who does not
  work on Brazil should not have to look up *improbidade*, *parecer
  prévio*, or *lista de antiguidade*. See general §6.
- **Distinguish institutional facts from research interpretation.**
  This section catalogs the rules; interpretation belongs in
  `discussion.md` or in the model.
- **Stay focused on what matters for the paper.** A complete institutional
  history of Brazilian courts belongs in a textbook, not in this section.
  Include only what the reader needs to interpret the empirical strategy
  and findings.
- **Anchor with primary sources, not secondary.** Cite the statute, not
  a textbook summary; cite the case, not a press release.
- **Acronyms spelled out on first use.** STF, CNJ, MPF, TCE, CGU — give
  the full name and a one-line gloss the first time each appears.
- **Triangular structure within the section.** Put the institutional
  features that bear on the identification strategy first; secondary
  detail later. The reader's question is "what does the design rely on?",
  not "tell me everything about Brazilian courts".
