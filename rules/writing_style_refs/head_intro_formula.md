# Keith Head — The Introduction Formula

**Original:** https://blogs.ubc.ca/khead/research/research-advice/formula
**Author:** Keith Head (UBC)
**Origin:** Head learned the formula from John Ries, who learned it from Jim Brander.

This is a paraphrased summary for offline reference. For the authoritative
text, see the URL above.

---

The formula has five sequential components for an economics paper
introduction:

## 1. Hook

Attract the reader's interest by telling them this paper relates to
something interesting. A topic gains interest through:

- **Mattering.** When the outcome rises or falls, people are hurt or
  helped.
- **Being puzzling.** It defies easy explanation.
- **Being controversial.** Some argue one thing while others argue another.
- **Being large or common.** Significant in scale or scope.

Avoid bait-and-switch tactics. Avoid justifying a topic merely because
others have written about it.

## 2. Question

State the research question clearly. By the end of the second paragraph,
include an explicit "This paper addresses the question…" sentence so
readers know what the paper investigates.

Trial analogy: after detailing the crime (hook), identify the perpetrator
and build the case. Readers should grasp a clean research question with
an anticipated resolution.

## 3. Antecedents

Identify the prior work that is critical for understanding the
contribution this paper will make. Discuss only essential intellectual
precursors. The challenge: credit previous research while showing its
limitations, without seeming dismissive.

## 4. Value-added

Describe approximately three contributions relative to prior work. This
section distinguishes your work from antecedents and may span up to three
paragraphs. The contribution paragraphs should differ from the question
paragraph by demonstrating how contributions build meaningfully on
antecedent research, not by restating the question.

## 5. Roadmap

Outline the organization of the paper. Customize to your project — name
specific landmarks (problems, solutions, results) the reader will
encounter, rather than generic structural descriptions.

---

## Additional advice

Head recommends writing the introduction first, then revising it
repeatedly as you develop other sections. The introduction should receive
the most editorial attention.

The formula has two benefits:

1. Minimizes uncertainty when writing the introduction.
2. Ensures conformance with the social norms surrounding how an
   economics-paper introduction should read.
